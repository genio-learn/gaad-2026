How we build Genio Notes

Accessibility is baked into how we build at Genio. That means we design for inclusivity from the ground up, never as an afterthought.

We know that compliance can feel like a moving target as standards shift and technology evolves. This is how we ensure that accessibility is at the heart of our processes:

  • Built-in vigilance: We are committed to maintaining compliance standards to 2027 and beyond. Features are tested for accessibility before they are released, and accessibility tests are part of our release pipeline.
  • Expert partnerships: We continue to work alongside accessibility leaders like Level Access to challenge findings and maintain the highest possible standards.
  • Internal champions: Our dedicated Accessibility Champions are embedded in our engineering teams, ensuring that every new feature is built with inclusion at its heart from the planning stage.

We hold ourselves to the same standard we’ve just asked you to apply. Every change to Genio Notes is tested with screen readers and keyboard navigation and checked automatically before it ships. Nothing goes live until it passes.

There’s a learning reason for this too. A student who’s fighting the tool has less attention left for the lecture. Accessible design removes that extra load, so students can focus on learning itself. That’s why accessibility and learning science sit side by side in everything we build, from note taking to study tools.

What to look out for when supporting students

A quick checklist, mapped to the barriers you just experienced. Try them on the tools your students are currently using.

  • ⌨️ Keyboard access

    Can a student reach and use every control without a mouse or touchscreen?

  • 🔊 Screen reader support

    Do images, buttons and form fields have labels that make sense read aloud?

  • 🎙️ Voice control

    Does the visible label on a button match its accessible name, so “click [name]” works?

  • 🔎 Zoom & reflow

    At 400% zoom, does content reflow cleanly instead of overlapping or cutting off?

  • 🌀 Motion & animation

    Can a student turn off non-essential motion if it triggers dizziness or migraines?

  • 💬 Plain, consistent language

    Is instructional text simple and predictable for students with cognitive or learning disabilities?

If a tool fails one, that’s not automatically a deal breaker. What matters is how the vendor responds when you ask about it.
