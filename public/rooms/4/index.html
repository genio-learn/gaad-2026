What this room simulates

Low vision and screen magnification. Many students with low vision zoom in to 200%, 400% or more to read. A well-built page rearranges itself to fit — this is called “reflow”.

In this room we’ve deliberately squeezed the button you need into a narrow column of tiny text, so at normal size it’s easy to miss.

Hint: The control you need is somewhere in this report, but the layout makes it hard to find at normal zoom. Try zooming in to 200% or more (Ctrl+= on Windows/Linux, Cmd+= on Mac) and watch what happens to the layout.
I can’t work this out — show me how

How to solve it

  1. Zoom in: Ctrl and + on Windows, or Cmd and + on a Mac. Keep going until you reach at least 200%.
  2. Watch the three columns stack on top of each other. Scroll down to the Priority Annotations section.
  3. Under the P1 — Action required note, select Acknowledge priority access.
  4. Press Ctrl/Cmd and 0 to reset the zoom afterwards.

On a phone the columns are already stacked — just scroll down to Priority Annotations.

Why this matters for students

Here, zooming in makes things better, because the page is built to reflow. On many sites it’s the opposite: zooming in squashes text, pushes buttons off the edge of the screen or forces sideways scrolling on every line. For a student with low vision, that can turn a five-minute task into a frustrating one — or make it impossible.
