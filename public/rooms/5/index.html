What this room simulates

Sensitivity to motion. Movement on screen can cause dizziness, nausea or migraines for students with vestibular disorders, and can make it very hard to concentrate for some students with ADHD, autism or a concussion. Most devices have a “Reduce motion” setting, and good websites respect it.

We’ve filled this room with movement on purpose. It only settles down once that setting is switched on.

Hint: Motion can make a page impossible to use — not just distracting, but genuinely inaccessible for people with vestibular disorders or sensory sensitivities. Enable Reduce Motion in your OS settings (System Settings → Accessibility on Mac; Settings → Accessibility → Visual effects on Windows).

How to solve it

  1. Turn on Reduce Motion:
    • Mac: System Settings → Accessibility → Reduce motion (under Display or Motion, depending on your macOS version)
    • Windows 11: Settings → Accessibility → Visual effects → turn off Animation effects
    • iPhone or iPad: Settings → Accessibility → Motion → Reduce Motion
    • Android: Settings → Accessibility → Remove animations (the wording varies by device)
  2. Come back to this page. The movement should stop — refresh the page if it doesn’t.
  3. Select Extract frequency code.

You can switch the setting back off afterwards.

Why this matters for students

For most visitors this room is mildly annoying. For a student with a vestibular disorder, a page like this can trigger symptoms that last long after they’ve closed it. The fix is small: respect the Reduce Motion setting, and give people a way to pause anything that moves.
