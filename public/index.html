Welcome to the Lab.
Things work differently here.

Students with disabilities run into barriers in digital tools every day. This challenge puts you in their shoes. Each room is deliberately broken for the way most people use a computer — and only works if you use the assistive technology or setting those students rely on.

Everything broken here is broken on purpose.

The rooms deliberately break accessibility guidelines so you can feel the frustration for yourself. They’re a simulation, not a mistake. See every issue we’ve built in, and why.

No prior experience with assistive technology needed. Each room explains what it simulates and gives you a hint. Try to feel the friction first — and if you get stuck, every room has an “I can’t work this out” button that shows you how.

How it works

  1. 1
    Enter each challenge room There are 5 rooms. Each one simulates a barrier faced by students with a different disability, and the way they get around it.
  2. 2
    Find the hidden clue Each room hides a one-word clue. Clicking around as usual won't reveal it — you'll need to interact the way that student would.
  3. 3
    Collect all five clues Jot them down. They'll make sense when you reach the final quiz.
  4. 4
    Answer the final quiz Combine your clues to unlock the answer and finish the challenge.
Which accessibility guidelines does this break?

Each room deliberately fails specific criteria from WCAG (the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, the international standard for accessible websites) — covering keyboard access, semantic structure, focus order, reflow and motion. These failures are intentional and limited to the challenge rooms. Visit the full list of intentional violations for details.

The five rooms

Pick any room to begin — you can tackle them in any order. Each one returns you here when you've found its clue.

  • ⌨️ Room 1 — Keyboard only

    Mouse? Useless. Find the clue using nothing but your keyboard.

    Simulates: students who can’t use a mouse — because of a physical disability, a tremor or sight loss — and rely on the keyboard.

    Keyboard navigation Enter room →
  • 🔊 Room 2 — Screen reader

    The visual layout is a dead end. The semantic layer tells a different story.

    Simulates: blind and partially sighted students who use a screen reader to hear the page read aloud.

    Screen reader Enter room →
  • 🎙️ Room 3 — Voice control

    Mouse is dead. Keyboard is a maze. One button has the name you need to say.

    Simulates: students with limited hand movement, pain or fatigue who control their computer by voice.

    Voice control Enter room →
  • 🔎 Room 4 — Zoom & magnification

    The control you need is there. The layout is hiding it. Zoom in and watch everything shift.

    Simulates: students with low vision who zoom in or magnify the screen to read.

    Zoom & reflow Enter room →
  • 🌀 Room 5 — Reduced motion

    The page is alive with interference. Calm the signal to extract the hidden code.

    Simulates: students for whom movement on screen causes dizziness, migraines or difficulty concentrating.
